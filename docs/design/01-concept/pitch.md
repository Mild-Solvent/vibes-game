# Pitch (one page)

**Everyone's watching. Nobody's ready.**

2–6 friends are the crew of a live show. The audience only sees what's on camera or
on stage, and **everything else is yours to ruin.** Run the cues, feed the lines,
swap the set, hide the fire. When the show ends, everyone watches **The Tape**: the
broadcast exactly as the audience saw it, with your panicked whispers leaking through
the mics.

## The hook in one sentence per mode
- **We're Live!**: You're the crew of a live evening news show. One of you is the anchor,
  and the teleprompter is typed live by your friend.
- **Places, Please!**: You're the backstage crew of a live play. Swap sets in the dark,
  pull the right rope, and don't let the audience hear you swear.

## Pillars
1. **Front vs. back.** Two worlds at once: the calm show and the panic behind it.
   Every gag lives in the gap between them.
2. **The show must go on.** There's no pause and no retake. The run-of-show clock is
   the only timer, so failure keeps going and turns into comedy.
3. **The Tape is the product.** Every round produces a watchable, exportable clip.
   Streamers get content for free, and viewers who've never played still understand it.
4. **Voice is the controller.** Proximity chat, the live mic, whispering, headset
   channels. How you talk is how you play.
5. **Fits in a sentence.** Anyone should be able to play within 30 seconds of joining.

## Why it can sell
- Nothing in the scan does "crew of a live show" (see research).
- Streamers are already a live show, so this is a show about their own job. They'll get
  it instantly.
- It's modular: each new "show" is a new mode on shared systems, which fits the
  update-driven, streamer-return pattern (see roadmap).

## Target
- 2–6 players online (Steam). Solo is technically possible but not the point.
- Price $10–13. Sessions last 8–15 min per show and 1–2 h per evening.
- PC first (Steam). Console later, if ever.
